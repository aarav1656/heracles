### The finding that shapes decision 3

Walk of the RHC SPY feed at block 75,602,686 (round id is `1 << 64 | n`):

| Round n | Answer | updatedAt (UTC) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 146 | 772.32802713 | Fri 09-25 16:03:00 | last print before Friday's 20:00 UTC close |
| 147 | 771.21266423 | Mon 09-28 00:00:40 | Sunday 20:00 ET reopen, 55.96 h later. No weekend heartbeat |
| 148 | 766.74004349 | Mon 09-28 14:44:26 | first round after the 13:30 UTC open |

AAPL shows the same shape: round 671 at Fri 19:49:25 (341.45318048), then 672 at Mon 00:00:48 (340.43260307), 52.19 h later, then 673 at Mon 13:37:33 (342.42873240). NVDA's and TSLA's recent rounds are 0.50% to 0.56% apart, one deviation step each.

`Treaty.setReference` takes the first round at or after Friday's close (20:00 UTC, `closeTs` 1,790,366,400), within `REFERENCE_MAX_DELAY` (36 h). On Robinhood Chain that first round is 147, at 1,790,553,640, which is 187,240 s (52.0 h) after close. `setReference` reverts `BadRound`, anyone can call `voidReference` with round 147, and the layer ends `Cancelled` with the premium refunded. AAPL gives the same result (round 672, 52.0 h after close). So on Robinhood Chain v2 pays nothing on any weekend with no after-hours print between 4:00 and 8:00 pm ET Friday: the buyer is never covered and writers never earn. On weekends that do have such a print, the reference becomes an after-hours price instead of the close.

Widening the bound past 52 h would be worse. Round 147 is the Sunday-night overnight open, so it already carries the weekend's news, which is exactly what P5 exists to stop. For a long 2,000,000 USDG SPY layer with a 5,000 USDG retention on this weekend:

- round in effect at close (v3): reference 772.32802713, loss 14,470.492960, payout 9,470.492960 USDG
- first round after close with no delay bound: reference 771.21266423, loss 11,598.929704, payout 6,598.929704 USDG, 30% short
- v2 as deployed: void, payout 0, premium refunded

The Sepolia SPY feed behaves differently. It prints a heartbeat every 24 h through the weekend (Fri 16:18:43, Sat 16:18:48, Sun 16:18:51, read at Sepolia block 313,931,466), which is why the rule looked right in testing.

## Decision 1: USDC alongside USDG

**Chosen: one book per asset.** The Treaty constructor takes `address[] assets`. For each asset it creates two Capacity vaults, a first-loss vault and a senior vault. `bind` takes a `book` index. The layer's collateral, premium and payout are all in that book's asset.

Why:
- Ensuro "currently accepts only USDC" (research doc, Ensuro LP FAQ). A USDG-only pool shuts out the top-ranked writer by construction. The Nexus-style route (a writer deposits, then buys Nexus cover on the deposit, as in the Nexus and Ensuro partnership) works the same way on a USDC vault.
- Both tokens are 6 decimals on every chain read (table above), and the pricer works in 6-decimal units. The constructor must revert unless every asset reads `decimals() == 6`. The pricer's comment says "USDG's native 6-decimal fixed point", but its math has no asset in it.
- Both tokens support EIP-2612 on Arbitrum Sepolia and USDG does on Robinhood Chain (domain separators recomputed above), so `depositWithPermit` works unchanged in every vault. The web hook must build the domain per token: USDC is ("USD Coin", "2"), USDG is ("Global Dollar", "1").

Depeg handling. No conversion happens anywhere, so a depeg moves value only between parties who chose that book. The loss is computed in USD from the equity feeds and paid as the same number of book-asset units, so a buyer who picks a book takes that stablecoin's USD basis on the payout. Writers in a book already hold that asset. A bind-time depeg guard (reject a bind when the asset's Chainlink USD feed reads below 0.99) is LATER. RHC has `USDC / USD` `0x9e6f...2546` and `USDG / USD` `0x61B7...9aD2`. Arbitrum Sepolia has `USDC / USD` `0x0153...9309`. A USDG/USD feed on Sepolia was not checked.

Which asset a claim pays in: the layer's book asset, always.

Share pricing and ERC-4626 semantics: every vault is today's `Capacity` with one change, a name and symbol passed to the constructor. `asset()`, `totalAssets()`, the entry and exit previews (F3), the per-cutoff gate (H1), `maxDeposit`/`maxMint`/`maxRedeem`, and `depositWithPermit` are unchanged. Each vault is a standard single-asset ERC-4626, so Ensuro, Nexus-wrapped writers or a curator can integrate it the way they integrate any vault.

Rejected:
- **One vault with per-asset accounting.** ERC-4626 has exactly one `asset()`. Pricing shares over two assets needs a USDC/USDG mark, which carries a depeg in either coin to every writer, and it is no longer a standard vault an integrator can read.
- **A wrapper that converts USDC to USDG on deposit.** There is no on-chain 1:1 route. Paxos mints and redeems through an off-chain account, DefiLlama tracks no USDG on Arbitrum One (research doc), and an AMM route adds slippage and depeg exposure to every deposit.
- **USDC only.** It gives up the USDG pool on Robinhood Chain (691.1M USDG `totalSupply()` at RHC block 75,614,222) and the hackathon's extra consideration for USDG.

Premium flow changes with books, to avoid double approvals. The buyer approves **Treaty**. Treaty pulls the whole premium into itself, then each vault pulls its share from Treaty through the existing `receivePremium(address(this), amount)`. Treaty gives each vault a max allowance in its constructor. `Capacity.receivePremium` does not change. Treaty's balance of every asset is zero after every call (new invariant). As a side effect this closes L1/F5 (a permit resetting a buyer's standing premium allowance): buyers now approve Treaty and writers approve vaults, so no spender carries both allowances.

## Decision 2: first-loss and senior tranches

Per-market pools stay ruled out. Weekend-gap correlation runs from 0.54 to 0.82 across the four markets, and pooling saves only 11% of expected loss (research doc, "Structure").

### Waterfall

At bind, for a layer with retention `R` and limit `L` in book `b`:

```
J = L * juniorShareBps / 10_000          juniorShareBps = 3_000, immutable per deploy
first-loss slice  = sub-layer (R,     J)       locked in book b's first-loss vault
senior slice      = sub-layer (R + J, L - J)   locked in book b's senior vault
```

At settle, with `payout = min(L, max(0, loss - R))` computed exactly as today:

```
payoutJunior = min(payout, J)
payoutSenior = payout - payoutJunior
```

The senior slice pays only once the first-loss slice has paid its full `J`. Per scenario, `min(J, max(0, loss - R)) + min(L - J, max(0, loss - R - J)) = min(L, max(0, loss - R))`, so the split never changes what the buyer receives.

### Pricing the split from the pricer's own table

Two calls to the unchanged `IPricer.quote`, in the same transaction, so the volatility scale is identical in both:

```
(pTotal, , ) = pricer.quote(exposure, feeds, R, L)
(pJunior, , ) = pricer.quote(exposure, feeds, R, J)
premium       = max(pTotal, L * minRateOnLineBps / 1e4)     unchanged buyer price
premiumJunior = pJunior
premiumSenior = premium - pJunior
```

Why this is the table's own split and not a new parameter:
- EL is a mean of per-scenario payouts, and the two slices add up per scenario, so EL adds up exactly.
- The two slices' payouts both rise with the same loss, so the worst 25 scenarios for the layer are also the worst 25 for each slice, and TVaR95 adds up too.
- The capital charge is linear in the limit.

So `quote(R, J) + quote(R + J, L - J) = quote(R, L)`, up to integer rounding. Measured on the deployed Stylus pricer at Sepolia block 313,932,575: 1,027.081393 + 649.640765 = 1,676.722158, exact to the base unit (table above). A Python replica of `price_core` (it reproduces the README's 1,676.722158 exactly) differs by at most 2 base units across the layers below. `pJunior <= pTotal` always holds: EL, the tail load and the capital charge are each no larger for a sub-layer. So `premiumSenior >= 0`, and when the 50 bps floor binds, the top-up goes to the senior slice. The floor binds on deep layers, and 70% of the locked capital sits in the senior vault.

The junior share of each layer's expected loss sets its share of the premium, as the brief asked. **Estimated**, from `evidence/gap-tables/*.gaps.txt`, vol scale 1.0x, 30% first-loss share. Expected return per year is `(premium - EL) x 52 / slice limit`, before variance.

| Layer (long) | Pays, weekends of 504 (first loss / senior) | First-loss share of EL | of premium | Expected return a year, first loss | senior |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPY 2M, 20k retention, 60k limit (README layer) | 36 / 9 | 62.7% | 61.3% | 106.3% | 31.9% |
| SPY 1M, 3% to 8% | 5 / 2 | 39.8% | 38.8% | 20.9% | 14.9% |
| NVDA 1M, 5% to 10% | 10 / 9 | 39.8% | 39.4% | 60.3% | 40.6% |
| SPY+NVDA+AAPL+TSLA 1M each, 200k retention, 200k limit | 6 / 4 | 40.7% | 40.0% | 33.7% | 22.6% |

Why 30%: the first-loss money that exists is small and wants 30%+ (NetNet nnUSDG, $1.04M at 34.89% net), while the senior-type money is large and wants about 4.5% (Steakhouse USDG, $504.9M at 4.48%). Morpho API figures are from the research doc. At 30%, $100k of first-loss capital supports $333k of limit. At 20% the first-loss slice takes 51.4% of the README layer's EL on 20% of its capital. At 50% it takes 78%. The share is immutable per deploy so a writer knows their slice before depositing. The buyer's price does not depend on it.

One honest limit belongs in the writer docs: "senior" here means the upper 70% of each layer, not a far-away attachment. On the README layer it still loses something on 9 of 504 weekends. A pool-level waterfall, where first-loss capital absorbs the pool's whole weekend loss before senior pays anything, gives a much safer senior. But it needs cross-vault loss transfers at settle and breaks the per-vault ledger proofs that C1 and H1 rest on. It is LATER.

Rejected split rules:
- **Fill first-loss capacity first** (`J = min(L, juniorFree)`): a writer cannot know their risk before depositing, small layers go entirely to first loss, the senior earns nothing at low use, and the split depends on bind order.
- **Split by EL share alone**: it drops the tail load and the capital charge, so the senior would be paid below its own `quote(R + J, L - J)`, the price the same pricer puts on that risk alone.
- **Buyer picks J per layer**: the buyer does not care (the total does not change) and writers would get a different slice every week.

### Bind rules per tranche

- `J > 0`, `L - J > 0`, `premiumJunior > 0`, `premiumSenior > 0`, else `BadParams`. This generalises the L2 fix: no vault can lock capital for zero premium, so no vault's gate can be closed for free.
- Both slices must fit their vault's free capacity (`amount <= totalAssets() - locked`, the existing `lock` check). The largest bindable limit shown in the UI is `min(juniorFree / 0.3, seniorFree / 0.7)`.

### Locking, gate and withdrawal timing per tranche

Unchanged per vault. Each vault buckets its locked capital by the layer's cutoff. Deposits and withdrawals close at Friday 19:30 UTC for any vault holding a slice of a layer with that cutoff, and reopen when every such layer has settled or been cancelled. Both vaults in a book hold a nonzero slice of every layer (rule above), so their gates open and close together. Outside the gate, a withdrawal is capped at free capacity. Neither tranche gets a longer notice period. That is not needed while every slice is fully collateralised and settles on the same Monday.

## Decision 3: Robinhood Chain

### Does the chain carry what Heracles needs?

Yes, with two gaps. All four tabled markets have Chainlink feeds (addresses above), USDG is native with permit, and Stylus is live (ArbWasm `stylusVersion()` 3 under ArbOS 116, the same ArbOS the Sepolia pricer was deployed under, per `docs/pricing.md`). The gaps: there is no sequencer uptime feed, and the equity feeds stop from Friday's last print to the Sunday 20:00 ET reopen with no weekend heartbeat (SPY 55.96 h, AAPL 52.19 h). The second gap breaks the reference rule, as shown above.

### Reference rule for v3 (both chains)

`setReference(id, i, roundId)` accepts only the round **in effect at Friday's close**:

1. `block.timestamp > closeTs`. Strict, so no round can still land at `closeTs` in the same block.
2. `roundId` is in the feed's current phase (`_latestInPhase`, unchanged), and its answer is in `(0, uint64.max]`.
3. `updatedAt(roundId) <= closeTs`.
4. Either `roundId == latestRoundId`, or `updatedAt(roundId + 1) > closeTs`. Within a phase this picks exactly one round.
5. `updatedAt(roundId) + MAX_ROUND_AGE >= closeTs`, with `MAX_ROUND_AGE = 26 hours`. Weekday heartbeats are 24 h plus about 36 s on RHC (SPY Thu 04:04:06 to Fri 04:04:29).

`voidReference(id, i, roundId)` accepts either proof, permissionlessly, after `closeTs`:
- **stale**: `roundId` meets 2 to 4 and fails 5 (the feed was silent for over 26 h before close);
- **phase**: `roundId == (currentPhase << 64) | 1` and its `updatedAt > closeTs`, so the in-effect round sits in an older, unreachable phase.

The two proofs and `setReference` exclude each other. `_anyMarketFixable` treats a market with no reference as still resolvable unless it is void. A caller who wants to cancel a stuck layer calls `voidReference` first. `REFERENCE_MAX_DELAY` is deleted.

What this buys. The reference can never be later than the close, so P5 (a reference that absorbs the weekend) cannot happen by construction on any feed schedule. The error bound becomes the feed's deviation threshold: while the network is live, the in-effect answer is within 0.5% of the true close. On Sepolia that costs 0.04% on the last weekend (Fri 16:18:43 answer 771.0150 against Saturday's 771.3425). On RHC it turns a voided weekend into a settled one, at the 9,470.49 USDG payout above. The 504-weekend table is measured from the 4:00 pm close, so the two now agree. `fix` stays as it is: the first round at or after Monday 9:30 ET, predecessor strictly before.

Rejected: a hybrid ("first round after close if within 4 h, else the round in effect") needs two rules and lets the caller pick which one applies. Moving the Monday boundary to the Sunday 20:00 ET reopen matches when RHC lenders actually liquidate, but it changes what the 504-weekend table measures, and no Sunday-overnight price history is in the tables.

### Liveness guard that replaces the sequencer-uptime feed

What a sequencer outage can actually do to Heracles:
- `setReference` and `fix` pick rounds from history by timestamp, so an outage can delay those calls but cannot change which round is valid. An outage across Monday's open delays Chainlink's first post-open round to the restart. That is basis noise neither side controls.
- `bind` reads live rounds for the volatility scale. A layer bound through force inclusion during a long outage prices off stale rounds, and a layer bound on a feed that is already dead becomes the F1 grief.
- `cancel` treats "no equity round after expiry" as a dead feed. During a long outage that is really a halted chain, which is the G3 race without a sequencer feed.

Guard, three parts:
1. **bind freshness.** Every market feed must satisfy `latest.updatedAt + MAX_ROUND_AGE >= block.timestamp`. Write it as an addition, not `block.timestamp - updatedAt`, so fork tests that warp backwards do not underflow. On RHC this blocks binds from Monday 00:00 UTC to the Sunday-night reopen print (00:00:40 UTC under EDT, about 01:00 UTC under EST), and on holiday Mondays until the feed prints. Both are acceptable.
2. **reference staleness.** Rule 5 above: the same 26 h bound, applied to the in-effect round.
3. **cancel liveness.** A new immutable `livenessFeed`, a Chainlink feed with a heartbeat through weekends. `cancel` requires `livenessFeed.latestRoundData().updatedAt >= L.expiry + MAX_ROUND_AGE`. That proves Chainlink could still write to this chain long enough after expiry that a live equity feed (24 h heartbeat) must have printed its post-expiry round. Without it, "no round after expiry" could be a halted chain. Choice: USDC/USD on both chains. RHC `0x9e6f4605...2546` prints Fri 15:38:12, Sat 15:38:26, Sun 15:38:52, Mon 15:39:19. Sepolia `0x0153002d...9309` prints Fri 11:39:27, Sat 11:39:30, Sun 11:39:33, Mon 11:39:37. `address(0)` disables it and is only allowed in unit tests.

`sequencerUptimeFeed` stays as an optional constructor argument, and Arbitrum One deploys still pass `0xFdB631F5...697D`. `_requireSequencerUp` is unchanged.

Rejected:
- **ArbSys or L1-block based liveness.** `ArbSys.arbBlockNumber()` and `block.number` give the current L2 and parent-chain heights. No precompile exposes when the sequencer last stopped, and a contract cannot see a gap without storing history on every call.
- **ETH/USD as the liveness feed.** On RHC it moves on 0.5% deviation with a 24 h heartbeat. It was 2.5 h stale at block 75,602,686 while USDC/USD prints on a steady daily heartbeat.
- **Tightening `fix` to a maximum delay after the open.** RHC rounds print only on 0.5% moves, so the first post-open round can come hours after the open (SPY 74 min on 09-28). A bound would void ordinary weekends.

### Deploy decision

- **Arbitrum Sepolia: deploy, MUST.** Live SPY feed, Circle USDC and Paxos USDG testnet tokens, the deployed Stylus pricer `0xbe99...1f88` reused unchanged (table ids `[2]`), `livenessFeed` Sepolia USDC/USD, `sequencerUptimeFeed` `address(0)`.
- **Robinhood Chain mainnet: prepare as MUST, deploy only on the user's sign-off.** The standing rule is testnet only until the user approves mainnet. RHC testnet 46630 has no equity feeds, so a testnet deploy would need replayed fixture feeds, which proves nothing about the chain's real feeds. MUST items: `script/DeployRobinhood.s.sol` (guards `block.chainid == 4663`, feeds SPY/NVDA/AAPL/TSLA from the table above, a USDG book only, `livenessFeed` RHC USDC/USD, `sequencerUptimeFeed` 0, 30% first-loss share, 50 bps floor), a Stylus pricer deploy command for the same bytecode with `table_ids = [2, 0, 1, 3]` (SPY, NVDA, AAPL, TSLA), and `test/RobinhoodWeekend.fork.t.sol` replaying the real 2026-09-25/28 weekend (below). With sign-off the deploy is contracts only, with no capital and no bind, because no weekend can settle before the deadline.
- **Arbitrum One: no deploy.** The Arbitrum One fork tests stay as the sequencer-feed path.


## Decision 4: buyer story

Order and wording for README, SUBMISSION and the Overview page. Every figure keeps its source and block.

1. **Lighter's LLP on Robinhood Chain, the lead buyer.** Lighter keeps RWAs trading through the weekend on internal pricing and "the internal price is going to instantly converge" when the oracle returns. The LLP takes over accounts below close-out margin (Lighter docs, quoted in the research doc). Size: Lighter Robinhood Perps $106,694,809 TVL (DefiLlama, 2026-09-29). LLP equity inventory at Friday close was not measured. State the basis risk plainly: Heracles pays on Chainlink's Friday-close round to its first Monday round after the open, while the LLP loses on Lighter's own weekend price.
2. **Stock-token lenders.** Morpho on Robinhood Chain: $569,486,958 TVL and $456,002,981 borrowed across the chain (DefiLlama, 2026-09-29), and about $675k borrowed against AAPL, NVDA, SPCX, GOOGL and SPY tokens at 62.5% LLTV (Morpho API snapshot, research doc). Aave V4's Equities Hub is on Base, where the feeds "hold the last published price" over weekends (Aave blog). At 62.5% LLTV the lender loses only on a gap near 37.5%, and the worst weekend in the tables is -14.9% (TSLA), so the lender segment buys a far-out-of-the-money bad-debt layer as its second use of the Treaty.

## Pricer: what changes and what does not

The deployed Stylus pricer (`0xbe99...1f88`, Sepolia) is reused **unchanged** for v3 on Sepolia. Tranche pricing is two calls to `quote()`, and additivity was checked against it live.

Places that must or should change:

| # | Where | Change | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | RHC deployment | Same bytecode, new `cargo stylus deploy` on chain 4663 with `table_ids = [2, 0, 1, 3]`. A deployment, not a code change | MUST if RHC is signed off |
| 2 | `contracts/src/IPricer.sol` comment | "USDG's native 6-decimal fixed point" becomes "the book asset's 6-decimal units". Comment only, no ABI change | MUST, with lane L2 |

| 4 | Loads | `RISK_LOAD_PPM` and `CAPITAL_CHARGE_ANNUAL_PPM` are constants. Ensuro re-prices live and desks price from implied vol (research doc). A governance-bounded load needs a pricer change and a new admin surface | LATER |
